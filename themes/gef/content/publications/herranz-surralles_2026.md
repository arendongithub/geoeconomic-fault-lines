The article examines whether and how the Global Gateway infrastructure projects are underpinned by geoeconomic goals and means. Leveraging an own-gathered database of 117 projects, the article conveys a mixed picture: while geoeconomic intent is partially visible in the choice of projects and effort in reinforcing state-market relations, other forms of liberal economic statecraft (in embedded and neoliberal variants) continue to prevail.
